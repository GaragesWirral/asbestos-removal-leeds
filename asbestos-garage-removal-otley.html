Otley's Stone Buildings, and the Asbestos Hidden in Their Roofs

Otley's market town character is defined by stone construction, Georgian and Victorian stone-built commercial and residential properties, and the stone outbuildings and garages that were added to many of them during the 20th century. The stone exterior of an Otley garage or outbuilding gives no indication of what the roof is made of, and in the majority of structures built or re-roofed between 1940 and 1985, the answer is asbestos cement sheets. Thomas Chippendale may have been born here in 1718, but the asbestos cement garage is a legacy of a very different era.

The market town character of Otley also means we encounter a number of outbuilding structures associated with older commercial or agricultural use, yards behind former trading premises, stone barns converted to garages, and outbuildings whose construction predates the asbestos era but were fitted with asbestos cement roofs during 20th-century improvements. We assess each structure individually before quoting.
