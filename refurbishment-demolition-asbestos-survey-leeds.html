What It Is

What Is a Refurbishment and Demolition Asbestos Survey?

A refurbishment and demolition asbestos survey finds the asbestos that building work is likely to disturb, before anyone starts. Under the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 and the HSE survey guide HSG264, this type of survey is needed before refurbishment or demolition of any building built before 2000.

Unlike a management survey, it is fully intrusive. Within the areas where work will happen, the surveyor lifts floor coverings, opens up stud walls and ceilings, breaks into boxing and ducts, and looks inside the building fabric. Demolition surveys cover the whole structure, including roof voids, underfloor spaces and service routes.

The goal is simple: no hidden asbestos for your builders to discover halfway through a job.

Typical reasons for this survey

  • Kitchen, bathroom or loft conversion in an older home
  • Rewiring, replumbing or new heating systems
  • Knocking through walls or removing chimney breasts
  • Office, shop and restaurant fit outs
  • Strip outs and conversions of mills and warehouses
  • Full or partial demolition
Why It Matters

Why a Management Survey Is Not Enough

A management survey only looks at what is reasonably accessible during normal use. Asbestos boards behind plasterboard, lagging inside a sealed boxing or tiles under a newer floor will often be missed, because the surveyor is not allowed to break into the building fabric.

That is exactly the material your builder will hit with a saw or hammer. Disturbing it releases fibres, puts workers and occupants at risk, and usually means stopping the job, cleaning up under controlled conditions and paying for a licensed contractor at short notice. A refurbishment survey costs a fraction of that.

For larger jobs, the client has a duty under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 to provide pre construction information, and asbestos information is a key part of it. Your survey report gives your contractors what they need to plan safely.

R&D Surveys

R&D Surveys, the HSE and CDM

You may see a refurbishment and demolition survey called an R&D survey, or by its older name, a Type 3 survey. They all mean the same thing: a fully intrusive and sometimes destructive asbestos survey of the areas where construction work will happen.

The survey is the client's responsibility. If you are paying for the building work, you should arrange the asbestos survey and pass the report to your builder or principal contractor before work starts. Builders should not start work in an older building until they have seen it.

Because a refurbishment and demolition survey has to reach every hidden material in the scope of works, the area usually needs to be vacated and cleared. That is why the survey is normally booked once your plans are settled but before the strip out begins. If the plans change later, any new areas need surveying too.

Why does this matter so much? Asbestos fibres are microscopic. When asbestos containing materials are disturbed, fibres are released into the air, and if you inhale them they can cause mesothelioma, lung cancer and asbestosis, diseases that can take decades to appear. This survey is fully intrusive and destructive by design, because you cannot rely on a non intrusive inspection to find what is hidden inside the building fabric.

The asbestos survey report identifies all asbestos containing materials (ACMs) within the scope, so they can be removed by a licensed contractor or trained team before the builders arrive. For demolition, all asbestos must be removed as far as reasonably practicable before the building comes down.

How It Works

How the Survey Works

Agreeing the scope. We start with your plans, drawings or a description of the work, so the survey covers every area that will be affected. A clear scope keeps the cost down and makes sure nothing is missed.

Preparing the building. Because the survey is intrusive, the areas being surveyed should be empty of people and, where possible, furniture. For homes we plan around you; for businesses we can survey in phases.

The inspection. The asbestos surveyor works methodically through each area, sampling suspect materials and recording their extent and condition. Samples are analysed by a UKAS accredited laboratory.

The report. You get a register of every material found within the scope, with locations, quantities, photographs and lab results, ready to hand to your builder or principal contractor.

If asbestos is found

Any asbestos that will be disturbed by the work should normally be removed before the work starts. Depending on the material, that may be licensed work carried out by an HSE licensed contractor, or non licensed work done by trained operatives.

Licensed work needs notification to the HSE before it starts, so allow time in your programme. Once the work is done, update your asbestos register and asbestos management plan if the building is non domestic, or keep the paperwork safe if it is your home.
