Morley in Focus

Why Morley Has a Significant Asbestos Challenge

Morley is one of the most historically layered towns in South Leeds. Its origins as a centre of woollen cloth production in the 18th and 19th centuries left behind a dense legacy of stone-built workers' terraces, mills, and commercial buildings. The 20th century added waves of council housing and private semis to this fabric — and with them, asbestos.

What makes Morley distinct from more purely residential suburbs is the sheer variety of its building stock. A single street in Morley town centre might contain Victorian stone terraces alongside 1960s infill semis and early 1980s council-built properties. Each era carries its own asbestos profile, and each requires a different approach when it comes to surveying and removal.

This variety is precisely why local expertise matters. A contractor who doesn't understand Morley's housing history is less equipped to know where to look, what to expect, and how to advise you on what needs doing. We've worked across Morley and the surrounding South Leeds area for years, and that experience is reflected in the quality of our surveys and the safety of our removals.

Morley's Three Housing Eras — and Their Asbestos Risk

Understanding when your property was built is the starting point for assessing asbestos risk.

Pre-
1940

Victorian & Edwardian Terraces

Morley's stone-built back-to-backs and through-terraces were not built with asbestos — but many were modernised through the 1950s–80s with ACM insulation, artex, and replacement roofing. The renovation layer is where the risk lies.

1945–
1975

Post-War Council & Private Estates

Morley's post-war building programme produced hundreds of council homes and private semis using asbestos as a matter of routine — in ceiling boards, partition walls, roofing, and pipe lagging. The highest concentration of ACMs in the area is found here.

1975–
1990

Late 20th Century Semis & Estates

Properties built in the final years before the asbestos ban can contain ACMs in specific locations — particularly artex ceilings, floor tiles, and garage roofing. Risk is lower but not zero. Survey before any structural work.

Asbestos Removal Morley — Your Questions Answered

Straight answers to what Morley homeowners and landlords ask us most.

The original Victorian and Edwardian terraces in Morley were built before asbestos was widely used, so the structure itself is unlikely to contain ACMs. The risk comes from subsequent renovation. Most of Morley's older terraces were updated during the 1950s, 60s, and 70s — when artex, vinyl floor tiles, asbestos insulation board, and asbestos cement were all commonplace renovation materials. A pre-war Morley terrace that was refitted in 1968 could easily contain multiple types of asbestos that have never been identified or removed. A professional management survey is the only way to find out.

For non-domestic premises — including HMOs and houses in multiple occupation — landlords have a legal duty to manage asbestos under the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012. This means identifying whether ACMs are present, assessing their condition, and putting a written management plan in place. For purely domestic rentals the duty to manage does not technically apply, but all landlords have a general duty of care to their tenants. We strongly recommend management surveys for all pre-1990 Morley rental properties — and can arrange these efficiently across multiple properties at competitive rates.

Costs depend on the type of material and the volume to be removed. As a guide: a domestic asbestos garage roof removal in Morley typically costs £500–£1,500. Artex removal is priced per square metre. A management survey for a standard three-bedroom Morley property usually starts from £200–£300. On completion of every licensed removal job, we provide: a clearance certificate confirming that air testing has passed and the property is safe to reoccupy or continue works in; a waste transfer note proving that all asbestos waste was disposed of at a licensed hazardous waste facility; and copies of the written plan of work and relevant risk assessments. These documents are important — keep them safely, as they will be relevant when you sell the property or apply for planning permission for future works.

For licensed removal of higher-risk materials inside the home — such as AIB or pipe lagging — the affected area will need to be vacated. In some cases, depending on the extent of the works, you may need to vacate the whole property for the duration.
