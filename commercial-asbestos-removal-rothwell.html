Asbestos Materials Most Common in Rothwell's Commercial Buildings

Rothwell's commercial building stock reflects its dual identity as a Victorian market town and a former coal mining centre. The town centre contains civic and commercial buildings from the late 19th century; the surrounding LS26 corridor has post-war light industrial premises associated with the mining era's support industries. Both types carry significant asbestos risk in different ways.

In Victorian civic and commercial buildings around Holy Trinity Church and the Town Hall, including the 1895 Town Hall itself, the characteristic asbestos finds are pipe lagging on central heating systems, artex ceilings in meeting rooms and commercial spaces, and asbestos floor tiles beneath later flooring layers. Post-war industrial units in the LS26 business corridor, many built during the colliery support era of the 1950s–70s, predominantly contain asbestos cement roof sheets and wall panels. AIB ceiling systems and fire doors are typical in the office areas of these industrial buildings. Oulton Hall and large hospitality premises of its era may contain sprayed asbestos coatings in service and plant areas.
