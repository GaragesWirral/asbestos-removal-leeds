Morley's housing ranges from stone-built terraces in the shadow of the 1895 Town Hall to inter-war semis on the leafy residential roads and newer private development on the town's outskirts. Whether you are planning an extension to an inter-war semi, need a pre-sale survey and clearance on an older property, or have found suspected asbestos in a garage roof on a post-war estate, Yorkshire Asbestos Solutions provides HSE-licensed residential removal across Morley and the wider LS27 area.

Morley is an ancient market town with a royal charter from the medieval period. It was the birthplace of Herbert Henry Asquith, Prime Minister from 1908 to 1916. The town grew through the woollen cloth trade and later coal mining, developing its own strong civic identity reflected in its Victorian Town Hall (1895) and Scatcherd Park. Morley became part of the City of Leeds in 1974 but retains a distinct character.

How Morley's Mixed Housing Eras Create Varied Asbestos Risk

Morley has a mix of pre-war terraces in the town centre, post-war council housing on outlying estates in Churwell, Gildersome, and Drighlington, and private suburban housing from the 1970s onwards. Victorian and Edwardian housing is concentrated around the town centre and older residential streets.

Properties built throughout Morley between the 1880s and the 1980s are likely to contain some form of asbestos-containing material. Victorian terraces near the town centre typically contain artex, floor tiles, and soffits. Post-war council housing and 1960s–70s extensions commonly have asbestos cement garage roofs and insulation board in ceiling systems.

Asbestos in Morley Homes: Common Finds by Property Type

Morley's housing stock is a mix of stone-built Victorian and Edwardian properties near the town centre, inter-war semis on the residential streets radiating from the high street, and post-war housing developments across LS27. Given that asbestos was used continuously in housebuilding from the 1920s through to 1999, almost any Morley property built before the late 1990s should be treated as potentially containing ACMs until surveyed.

  • Artex ceilings and textured wall coatings, near-universal in Victorian and Edwardian Morley terraces renovated in the 1960s–80s; also common in 1970s–80s new builds
  • Asbestos floor tiles, beneath carpets and vinyl in inter-war and post-war kitchens, hallways, and bathrooms
  • Asbestos cement garage roofs and soffits, standard on detached garages and fascia boards built before 1985
  • Pipe lagging, on original heating and hot water pipework in Victorian stone terraces near the town hall and high street
  • Asbestos insulation board, in the airing cupboards, ceiling tiles, and fireplace surrounds of 1950s–70s housing

Morley Town Hall (1895) stands at the heart of an ancient market town that was recorded as more valuable than Leeds itself in the Domesday Book of 1086. Scatcherd Park, with its Victorian bandstand, occupies the elevated ground above the town centre within walking distance of residential streets where inter-war semis and Victorian terraces share postcodes. Morley Heritage Centre and St Peter's Church on Queen Street stand among the streets of housing built to serve the town's textile and civic industries, and it is precisely the renovation works carried out on these properties during the 1960s–80s that introduced the artex coatings, floor tiles, and insulation board that we routinely find and remove in LS27 today.
