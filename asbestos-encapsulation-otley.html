Otley's Georgian and Victorian Properties, Encapsulation as the Sensitive Approach

Otley's market town character is defined by stone-built Georgian and Victorian commercial and residential properties, many of which are part of the town's historic streetscape. When asbestos-containing materials are found in these buildings, almost always from 20th-century renovation works rather than from the original construction, the case for encapsulation over removal is often strengthened by the age and character of the building fabric. Removing artex from the ceiling of a Georgian town house or Victorian listed property carries a real risk of damage to original plasterwork and cornices. A penetrating encapsulant achieves the compliance outcome without that risk.

The market town character of Otley also creates encapsulation requirements in commercial premises. Properties on the market place and surrounding streets that are in commercial use, cafés, shops, professional offices, often have asbestos-containing materials in their interiors from renovation eras spanning the 1940s to the 1980s. For commercial landlords with a duty to manage under CAR 2012, encapsulation of intact ACMs in occupied commercial spaces is a particularly attractive option where full removal would require closing the premises.
