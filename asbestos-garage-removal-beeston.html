Beeston's Post-War Garage Blocks, Built at the Peak of Asbestos Cement Use

The council estates built across Beeston Hill and Middleton in the 1950s, 60s, and 70s included garage blocks as a standard feature, and those garage blocks were built when asbestos cement was the universally-specified roofing material for this type of structure. Virtually every garage block on these estates has an asbestos cement roof; a large proportion has asbestos cement side wall panels too. After decades of weathering, many of these sheets are now at an age where deterioration is accelerating and removal is becoming necessary.

Right-to-buy homeowners in Beeston who have purchased their council properties often inherit asbestos cement garage structures along with the house, and discover, when they plan improvements, that the garage needs to come down before work can progress. We deal with this situation regularly in LS11 and understand the specific construction of Beeston's estate-era garages.

Shared Garage Blocks, Co-ordinating Removal With Neighbours

One feature specific to council estate garage blocks, as opposed to individually built garages, is that the asbestos cement roof often spans two or more adjacent units. In this situation, full removal of the shared roof requires the co-operation of all unit holders. We advise on the best approach at the site assessment, and where a joint removal programme is the most cost-effective solution, we can help you coordinate with neighbouring owners or the remaining social landlord.

For individual right-to-buy garage units that share a roof structure with properties still managed by the local authority, we also work directly with housing teams and managing agents to coordinate compliant removal across mixed-ownership blocks.

Pricing and How to Book for Beeston
