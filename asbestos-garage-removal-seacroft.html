Seacroft's 1960s Planned Estate, Garage Blocks Built at the Peak of Asbestos Use

Seacroft was built in the 1960s and 70s as a self-contained satellite town within east Leeds, and its garage blocks were built as part of the same planned programme, using the construction materials standard for that era. That means asbestos cement roofing and often asbestos cement wall panels on virtually every garage block in the estate. Seacroft is one of the highest-concentration areas for asbestos cement garages in east Leeds, simply because the whole estate was built at once, in the same period, to the same specification.

With a significant right-to-buy take-up in Seacroft over the past four decades, many of these garage blocks are now in mixed ownership, some units privately owned, some still managed by the local authority or a housing association. We work with both private homeowners and housing organisations on Seacroft garage removal programmes, managing the coordination required for shared structures where different owners need to agree on timing and approach.

Shared Roof Structures, How We Handle Multi-Unit Seacroft Garage Blocks

The garage blocks on Seacroft's estate were built as continuous structures, a single roof over a row of four, six, or eight individual units. Removing one unit's asbestos cement roof in isolation is sometimes possible, but is often more disruptive and more expensive per unit than a shared programme. We advise on the most cost-effective approach at the site assessment and will be clear about which elements can be done individually and which benefit from being done together.

For housing associations and local authority properties in Seacroft, we can provide programme pricing for full block removal across multiple garage structures, reducing per-unit cost and minimising the number of site mobilisations required.
