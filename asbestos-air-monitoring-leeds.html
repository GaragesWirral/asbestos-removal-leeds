What It Is

What Is Asbestos Air Monitoring?

Asbestos air monitoring measures how many fibres are in the air in a room or work area. A pump draws a measured volume of air through a filter, and the filter is examined under a microscope by a trained analyst to count the fibres collected.

Air testing does not tell you whether a material contains asbestos. That is what sampling and surveys are for. What it does tell you is whether fibres have been released into the air you breathe, which is the thing that actually matters for health.

It is used in four main situations: before work starts, during removal, after an incident, and at the end of licensed removal work as part of the clearance process.

When air monitoring helps

  • After asbestos has been drilled, broken or damaged
  • Reassurance for staff, parents, tenants or residents
  • Background levels before removal starts
  • Leak checks around an enclosure during removal
  • Personal monitoring of operatives' exposure
  • Four stage clearance after licensed removal
Types of Testing

The Main Types of Asbestos Air Testing

Reassurance air testing. Carried out after an incident, such as a contractor drilling into an asbestos board, or after non licensed work, to check fibre levels in the area before people return. It is often the fastest way to calm a worrying situation with facts.

Background monitoring. Taken before removal work starts, to record normal fibre levels in and around the area.

Leak and boundary monitoring. Taken outside an enclosure during licensed removal to check that fibres are not escaping into the rest of the building.

Personal monitoring. Samplers worn by the people doing the work, to confirm their exposure stays well below the legal control limit and that their protective equipment is suitable.

Clearance air testing. The final air test at the end of licensed removal, carried out as part of the four stage clearance.

Clearance

Four Stage Clearance After Licensed Removal

When licensed asbestos removal work is finished, the area has to pass a four stage clearance before the enclosure is taken down and the space is handed back. HSE guidance requires it to be carried out by an independent analyst from an organisation accredited by UKAS for the work, not by the removal contractor.

  • Stage 1: a preliminary check of the site condition and the completeness of the job
  • Stage 2: a thorough visual inspection inside the enclosure to confirm no visible asbestos debris or dust remains
  • Stage 3: clearance air monitoring inside the enclosure, with the air disturbed, to confirm fibre levels are below the clearance indicator
  • Stage 4: a final assessment of the area after the enclosure has been dismantled

When the area passes, the analyst issues a certificate of reoccupation. Keep it with your asbestos records.

The method behind most asbestos air testing in the UK is set out in the HSE analysts guide, HSG248. Fibres are counted using phase contrast microscopy. For clearance, the result must be below the clearance indicator of 0.01 fibres per millilitre of air. For workers, the legal control limit is 0.1 fibres per cubic centimetre of air averaged over four hours, and good practice keeps exposure far below that.

Who carries it out?

Air monitoring and clearance testing must be carried out by competent, independent analysts. For licensed removal jobs we arrange an independent UKAS accredited analyst as part of the job, so you do not have to find one yourself.

For reassurance testing after an incident, call us first. We can advise whether air testing is the right next step, arrange it with an accredited analyst, and help with any clean up or removal that the results show is needed.

If asbestos has been disturbed

  • Stop work immediately and keep everyone out of the area
  • Close doors and switch off any fans or ventilation drawing air from the room
  • Do not sweep, vacuum or try to clean up dust or debris
