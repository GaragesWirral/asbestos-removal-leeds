Morley Has Three Distinct Eras of Asbestos Garage, All Needing Expert Removal

Morley's housing stock spans three eras that each produced different types of asbestos garage structure. The Victorian and Edwardian terraces near the Town Hall and Scatcherd Park have asbestos in rear outbuilding roofs added during 20th-century improvements. The inter-war semis on the residential roads often have single-skin brick garages built in the 1950s–60s, at the height of asbestos cement's use for domestic garage construction. And the post-war council and private estates carry the familiar garage blocks with full corrugated asbestos cement sheets. Each requires the same licensed removal process, though the scale and access vary.

Pre-sale removal is a regular reason we attend Morley properties. Inter-war semis in Morley change hands frequently and estate agents report that asbestos cement garage roofs are increasingly flagged by buyers' surveyors as needing remediation before completion. Our clearance certificates are formatted to satisfy conveyancing requirements and can be sent to your solicitor on the day the job is complete.

The Garage Removal Process We Use in Morley

We begin with a free site visit to Morley to assess the garage, confirm access from the road or rear entry, and check the condition of the sheets. Older asbestos cement that has weathered badly or grown algae is at higher risk of releasing fibres and is treated more carefully. On the day of work, the area is cordoned off; sheets are removed one at a time, kept intact where possible, wrapped in polythene, labelled as hazardous waste, and loaded for transport to a licensed facility.

You receive a waste consignment note on completion confirming legal disposal. If you need the walls left standing for a new roof, we leave them clear and ready. If the full structure needs to come down, we clear everything and leave the area ready for repurposing, whether that is a new garage build, a garden project, or simply the return of usable outdoor space.
