Asbestos Materials Most Common in Seacroft's Commercial Buildings

Seacroft presents an almost unique asbestos challenge in the Leeds district: the vast majority of its commercial and community buildings were constructed in a single concentrated period, the 1960s and early 1970s, at the absolute peak of asbestos use in British construction. This means the risk density in older Seacroft buildings is higher than in areas with more mixed building ages.

In Seacroft's 1960s–70s schools, community centres, and civic buildings, asbestos insulation board (AIB) is the dominant concern, used in ceiling tile systems, partition walls, fire doors, service cupboards, and around structural columns. AIB is one of the highest-risk forms of asbestos material and requires licensed removal. Artex and textured coatings were applied to virtually all domestic and commercial ceilings built in this era. Asbestos floor tiles with bitumen adhesive are found beneath almost all floor coverings in 1960s–70s public buildings. Asbestos lagging on heating system pipework survives in plant rooms and ceiling voids of these buildings. Pre-refurbishment surveys are essential before any work in Seacroft's older commercial estate.
