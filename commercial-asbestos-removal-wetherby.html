Wetherby's strategic position on the A1, one of England's great coaching and commercial routes since medieval times, gives it a commercial property market that includes town-centre retail and professional services, hospitality and conference facilities serving the A1 corridor, and light industrial units on the business parks that have developed on the town's outskirts. Whether you manage a commercial property in Wetherby town centre or a business park unit in LS22, Yorkshire Asbestos Solutions provides licensed commercial asbestos removal with full compliance documentation.

Wetherby's commercial history is one of the most distinguished in the Leeds district. A staging post on the Great North Road for centuries, the town had up to 40 inns and alehouses at the height of the coaching era. The Thursday market, granted by Royal Charter to the Knights Templar in 1240, still runs today, making Wetherby one of the oldest continuously-trading market towns in the region. The Georgian Town Hall (1845), The Shambles, and the many listed buildings along the high street represent a commercial heritage spanning nearly 800 years. Wetherby Racecourse, one of the premier jump racing venues in the north, adds a major commercial leisure attraction to the town.

Local knowledge: Wetherby Racecourse, one of the North's premier National Hunt venues, is a major commercial entertainment facility that draws thousands of visitors each year. Asbestos Materials Most Common in Wetherby's Commercial Buildings

Wetherby is defined by its Georgian and Victorian commercial heritage, coaching inns, town hall, The Shambles, and centuries of market-town commerce. But the asbestos risk in Wetherby's commercial buildings is largely concentrated not in the original historic fabric, but in the successive layers of renovation and modernisation applied to these buildings during the 20th century.

In Georgian and Victorian coaching inns, hotels, and high-street commercial buildings, artex and textured coatings, applied during the 1960s–80s modernisation of these premises, are the most common asbestos find, typically on ceilings in public rooms, function spaces, and corridors. Asbestos floor tiles with bitumen adhesive are regularly found under later floor coverings, particularly in back-of-house and commercial kitchen areas. Pipe lagging on heating systems survives in roof voids and service areas. Wetherby Racecourse, with its mix of Victorian grandstand structures and later additions, carries risk from AIB in later-built enclosures and asbestos cement roofing on outbuildings. Post-war schools and care homes in LS22 contain the standard AIB and artex profile.
