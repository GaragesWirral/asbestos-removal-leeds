Commercial Asbestos Removal in Kirkstall, From Abbey Road Retail to Canal-Side Industry

Kirkstall's commercial landscape is anchored by its Abbey Road retail and mixed-use premises, the Kirkstall Forge development on the river corridor, and the light industrial and warehouse units that border the Leeds and Liverpool Canal. Whether you are managing a commercial property near the abbey, a landlord letting office space on Abbey Road, or a contractor working on the Kirkstall Forge regeneration site, Yorkshire Asbestos Solutions provides licensed commercial asbestos removal and surveys across LS5.

Kirkstall's commercial history is inseparable from its industrial past. The Leeds and Liverpool Canal and the River Aire made Kirkstall a hub for industrial activity from the 18th century, and the legacy of this can be seen in the range of commercial and industrial buildings that line the main arterial roads. The area around Abbey Road contains Victorian and Edwardian retail premises, while former industrial buildings have been converted for commercial use. The Kirkstall Forge site, claimed as Britain's longest continuously-used industrial site, represents the apex of this industrial heritage.

Local knowledge: Kirkstall Forge, set beside the River Aire near Kirkstall Abbey, claims to be the longest continuously-used industrial site in Britain, with origins in a 13th-century mill built by Cistercian monks. Its long industrial history is emblematic of the area's deep commercial heritage.

Asbestos Materials Most Common in Kirkstall's Commercial Buildings

Kirkstall's commercial building stock is shaped by its industrial past, canal-side warehouses, former engineering works, and Victorian retail buildings along Abbey Road sit alongside more recent commercial conversions. The Kirkstall Forge site and the surrounding canal corridor contain some of the most industrially significant, and therefore potentially most asbestos-intensive, premises in north-west Leeds.

Canal-side industrial and warehouse buildings in Kirkstall are most commonly found to contain asbestos cement roof sheets and cladding panels, the default external material for industrial buildings from the 1950s through to the mid-1980s. Sprayed asbestos coatings on structural steelwork are present in older engineering and factory buildings. In office and welfare areas within these industrial premises, AIB ceiling tiles and partition boards are typical. Victorian retail and commercial buildings along Abbey Road carry the usual profile of artex coatings, asbestos floor tiles, and pipe lagging from post-war renovation periods.
