Asbestos Materials Most Common in Garforth's Commercial Buildings

Garforth's commercial building stock reflects its transition from a coal mining village to a modern commuter town. Older buildings associated with the colliery era sit alongside post-war light industrial and retail premises, and both types carry specific asbestos risk profiles that differ substantially from each other.

Industrial and light manufacturing premises on Garforth's trading estate, including manufacturing facilities built in the 1960s–80s, most commonly contain asbestos cement roof sheets and cladding panels, which were the standard external material for industrial construction of that era. AIB ceiling tiles and partition boards are found in office, canteen, and welfare areas within these buildings. Post-war school and community buildings in Garforth carry significant risk from AIB in fire doors, ceiling systems, and service cupboards. Older colliery-era commercial buildings may contain asbestos lagging on pipes and boilers. Town-centre retail premises built in the 1960s–80s typically contain artex coatings and asbestos floor tiles.
