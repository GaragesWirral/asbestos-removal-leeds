Asbestos Surveying in Leeds

Find Out Exactly What Is in Your Building

If your Leeds property was built before 2000, there is a real chance it contains asbestos somewhere. In most cases it is harmless if left alone. The danger comes when it is drilled, cut, sanded or broken during building work, or when it is damaged and starts to deteriorate.

An asbestos survey in Leeds answers three simple questions: is asbestos present, where is it, and what condition is it in? With that information you can plan building work safely, meet your legal duties and decide whether anything actually needs to be removed.

We carry out asbestos surveying for homes, rented houses and flats, shops, offices, schools, warehouses and industrial units throughout Leeds, from the city centre to Otley, Wetherby, Garforth and Morley. Every sample we take is analysed by a UKAS accredited laboratory.

Management or Refurbishment?

The Two Main Survey Types Explained

The Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 and the HSE survey guide, known as HSG264, describe two main kinds of asbestos survey.

An asbestos management survey is for a building in normal use. The surveyor inspects all reasonably accessible areas, including above ceiling tiles and inside service cupboards, and samples suspect material. It locates anything that could be damaged or disturbed during everyday use and minor maintenance, and it produces an asbestos register for the building.

A refurbishment and demolition survey is needed before any building work that will disturb the fabric of the building. These surveys are intrusive. The surveyor lifts floors, opens up walls and ceilings and breaks into boxing within the areas where work will take place, so the rooms usually need to be empty. A management survey is not enough before refurbishment or demolition.

What a survey covers

  • A visual inspection of every area within the agreed scope
  • Sampling of suspect material, analysed by a UKAS accredited lab
  • Asbestos containing materials (ACMs) identified, located and photographed
  • A condition and risk assessment for each item found
  • Clear recommendations: leave and manage, repair, encapsulate or remove
  • An asbestos register you can give to builders and contractors

Areas that cannot be reached, such as a locked room or a sealed void, are listed in the report so you know exactly what was and was not inspected.

The Law

Do You Legally Need an Asbestos Survey in Leeds?

It depends on who you are and what you are doing.

Businesses and building owners. If you are responsible for maintaining or repairing non domestic premises, you have a legal duty to manage asbestos under the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012. In practice that means finding out whether asbestos is present, assessing the risk, keeping an asbestos register and a management plan, and telling anyone who might disturb it. The duty to manage asbestos also covers the common parts of flats, HMOs and shared houses, such as hallways, stairwells, lift shafts and plant rooms.

Anyone planning building work. Before refurbishment or demolition on any building built before 2000, including homes, the people doing the work need to know whether asbestos is present. A refurbishment and demolition survey of the affected area is the normal way to do this.

Homeowners. There is no legal requirement to survey your own home if you are simply living in it. But if you are about to start building work, are buying an older house, or have found a suspect material, a survey or a simple test gives you peace of mind and protects your family and your builder.

If you are a landlord across Leeds and you are unsure where you stand, call us. We will explain your responsibilities in plain English.

How It Works

From Your Call to Your Asbestos Survey Report

1. Tell us about the property. Age, size, what it is used for and what work you are planning. That tells us which survey type you need.

2. Fixed price and a date. We confirm the scope in writing so you know exactly which areas the survey covers and what it costs.

3. The inspection. The surveyor works through every area in scope, taking small samples of suspect material. Sample points are sealed afterwards. A house usually takes a few hours; larger buildings take longer.

4. Lab testing. Samples go to a UKAS accredited laboratory for analysis.

5. Your asbestos report. You receive a written report with the register, photographs, plans where needed, the lab results and clear recommendations for each item.

How much does an asbestos survey cost in Leeds?

The price depends on the type of survey, the size of the building and how many samples are needed. A single sample from a ceiling is a very different job from a refurbishment survey of a three storey office. We quote a fixed price once we know the scope, and we tell you upfront if extra samples might be needed.

If you only need to know about one material, a simple sample test is the cheapest option. If you are planning building work, paying for the right survey now is far cheaper than stopping a job halfway through because asbestos has been found.

Choosing a surveyor

Whoever you use, ask whether their surveyors hold the BOHS P402 qualification or equivalent, whether the company is UKAS accredited for surveying, and which laboratory analyses the samples. A good surveyor will be happy to answer.

Because we also carry out removal, we are open about it: a survey that finds asbestos in good condition will recommend leaving it in place and managing it. We would rather you trusted the report than paid for removal you do not need.

Common Finds

Where Asbestos Turns Up in Leeds Properties

Leeds has a huge stock of Victorian terraces, interwar semis, postwar council estates and older commercial buildings. These are the materials our surveys find most often:

  • Textured coatings such as Artex on ceilings and walls
  • Vinyl and thermoplastic floor tiles and bitumen adhesives
  • Asbestos insulation board in cupboards, soffits, fire doors and ceiling tiles
  • Cement garage roofs, gutters, downpipes and flues
  • Pipe lagging and boiler insulation in cellars and plant rooms
  • Roof sheets and cladding on warehouses and industrial units
